> **SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION POLICY.** This document was written for a technical
> demonstration of the Governed Banking Policy Assistant. It describes a fictional
> institution, Meridian Retail Bank, and does not reproduce, summarise or represent
> the policy of any real bank. It must not be relied on for any operational purpose.

# 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 This policy sets out how staff handle a customer dispute about a card
transaction on a debit or credit card issued by Meridian Retail Bank.

1.2 It covers unauthorised transactions, transactions where goods or services were
not received, incorrect amounts, and duplicate charges.

1.3 It does not cover card fraud investigations after a confirmed compromise, which
are governed by the Fraud Escalation Policy (FRAUD-ESC-002).

# 2. Standard procedure for lodging a dispute

2.1 Confirm the customer's identity in line with standard verification steps before
discussing any transaction detail.

2.2 Capture the transaction date, merchant name, amount, card number last four
digits, and the customer's description of what went wrong.

2.3 Advise the customer to attempt to resolve the matter with the merchant first for
non-receipt and incorrect-amount disputes, unless the customer states the
transaction was unauthorised.

2.4 Lodge the dispute in the disputes system within one business day of the
customer's contact. Provide the customer with the dispute reference number.

2.5 Tell the customer the standard assessment window is 21 calendar days for
domestic transactions and 45 calendar days for international transactions.

# 3. Time limits

3.1 Disputes must be lodged within 90 calendar days of the transaction date.

3.2 A dispute lodged after 90 days may still be recorded, but staff must tell the
customer the scheme time limit has passed and the claim is unlikely to succeed.

3.3 Time limits are not extended by staff. Any request to lodge a claim outside the
scheme window is a consequential action under section 5.

# 4. Provisional credit

4.1 A provisional credit may be applied while an unauthorised-transaction dispute is
assessed, where the disputed amount is 500 AUD or less and the customer has no
disputed claim declined in the previous 12 months.

4.2 A provisional credit is reversible. Staff must tell the customer the credit may
be reversed if the dispute is unsuccessful.

# 5. Consequential actions requiring human approval

5.1 The following actions must not be completed by frontline staff acting alone.
Each requires review and approval by the named authority before the action is taken.

| Action | Approval authority |
|---|---|
| Provisional credit above 500 AUD | Disputes Team Leader |
| Any permanent write-off of a disputed amount | Disputes Manager |
| Reversing a merchant chargeback already settled | Disputes Manager |
| Lodging a claim outside the 90-day scheme window | Disputes Team Leader |
| Waiving a card replacement or dispute-handling fee | Branch or Contact Centre Manager |
| Closing a dispute against the customer's stated wishes | Disputes Manager |

5.2 Approval must be recorded in the disputes system against the dispute reference
before the action takes effect. Verbal approval alone is not sufficient.

# 6. Customer communication

6.1 Confirm the outcome of a dispute to the customer in writing within five business
days of the decision.

6.2 Where a dispute is declined, tell the customer the reason and their right to
lodge a complaint under the Complaints Handling Policy (COMP-HAND-004).

# 7. Matters not covered by this policy

7.1 This policy does not state anything about the following, and staff must not
infer an answer from it:

- Tax treatment of a refunded or written-off amount.
- Legal advice on a customer's contractual rights against a merchant.
- Card scheme rules, interchange rates or scheme-level dispute economics.
- Business or commercial card products, which follow a separate procedure.
- Any transaction on an account held at another institution.

7.2 Where a question falls into 7.1, staff must not improvise an answer. Refer the
question to the policy owner named in this document.
